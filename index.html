The ecosystem

Everything between logging in and logging out.

Lucid began as a shell around your windows. Now the files, the pictures, the screenshots, the icons, the pointer and the login screen are its own too, and they all belong together.

Its own apps

Opened from the dock, drawn by the shell.

Lumen

A file manager of Lucid's own, and the Open and Save dialog of every app on the machine. Tabs you pin, colour and drag into windows of their own, bookmarks, your drives, a preview pane, and shares on other computers with Connect to server.

Lens and Settings

Lens opens a picture with its whole folder behind it, edits it without ever losing the original, and plays the videos beside it. Settings runs the rest of the machine, from displays and sound to accounts and the login screen, with a search that finds any option.

LucidShot

Print freezes the screen under one toolbar: a screenshot, a recording, the text in any part of the screen, or a colour off it. Drag a region or click a window, mark it up, pin it over your windows, or drag it straight into a chat.

Yours to arrange

Set it up where you see it.

Edit mode shades the wallpaper and leaves everything in reach: drag modules along the bar, float the dock or sink it into the edge, and drop widgets from the panel. The control centre gets an editor of its own.

Music

Whatever plays comes with its words.

The media panel scrolls with the song, the line being sung springing up and filling with colour, and the Lyrics widget sings along on the desktop: word by word, as karaoke, or the whole song going by.

Sounds of its own

Hear it.

Every sound in Lucid is synthesised for it, FM bells, plucks and blips in one key and one loudness. These are the files the shell plays.

Colour

Wear any palette.

Lucid builds a Material 3 palette from your wallpaper, or wears a fixed one, in dark or light, and hands it to GTK, Qt, kitty, Zen and VSCodium too. Pick one and this page wears it, icons included. Click a colour to copy it.

Install

Three commands.

Arch Linux and Hyprland 0.55 or newer. In a Wayland session the installer opens a window of its own, backs up whatever it replaces, and asks before it touches your system.

~ ❯ cd lucid
